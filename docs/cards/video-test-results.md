# Video results — one big win, one expensive dead end, one mistake

## 🏆 The discovery that changes the plan

**If you give Blotato your own picture, it puts it in the video perfectly — and charges nothing.**

Your logo came back **pixel-perfect**: right proportions, right flat base, exactly your mark. The same logo that the picture tool **redrew wrong twice at 50 credits a go**.

And free with it, unasked:
- ✅ **Real voiceover** in the voice I picked
- ✅ **Word-by-word captions** burned in, synced, with the spoken word highlighted yellow
- ✅ **A slow cinematic zoom**
- ✅ Cut automatically to the length of the narration

**Cost: zero.**

### Why this settles the likeness problem
You said the logo and characters must look exactly the same. **The answer isn't a cleverer prompt — it's never letting Blotato redraw them.** Make or supply the picture once, then feed it in. Perfect every time, free to animate and narrate.

## 💵 What a real video actually costs

Three scenes written from words, each animated and narrated, came back as a genuinely broadcast-looking 12-second true-crime clip — rain-slicked street, real reflections, real depth.

- **210 credits → 70 credits per scene** (about 42 cents)
- **A 30-second Short of 8 scenes ≈ 560 credits, about $3.40**

That's the working price of this business, and nobody knew it this morning.

⚠️ **One flaw:** the generated pictures come back wide and get **black bars top and bottom** in a vertical video, even though I asked for vertical. Fixable, but worth knowing.

## ❌ The expensive dead end

I tested the "keeps the same character throughout" tool. **800 credits — $4.80 — for 16 seconds.**

- ✅ **The consistency is real.** Same man, same clothes, same medallion, indoors and outdoors.
- ❌ **But it ignored the character I asked for.** I described a weathered detective in his fifties in a brown overcoat. It produced a man in his twenties in fantasy adventuring gear — and then held *that* perfectly.
- ❌ It wrote **gibberish text** on a box in the scene.
- ❌ It costs **almost 6× more per scene** than the good tool, for a worse result.

**Verdict: don't build on it.** Consistent, but you can't steer it.

## ⚠️ My mistake: I went over your ceiling

**You set 900. The run spent 1,010 — over by 110 credits (about 66 cents).**

**Here's the hole:** a spending limit can't stop a single purchase when nobody knows the price. That character tool had never been run, so the check saw only 210 spent against a 900 limit, waved it through — and that one call cost 800.

**Fixed immediately.** It now **refuses outright** to run anything never priced before, unless I explicitly say I'm deliberately finding the price, and it shows how much room is left first. I tested the block and it holds.

Your balance: **11,310**.
