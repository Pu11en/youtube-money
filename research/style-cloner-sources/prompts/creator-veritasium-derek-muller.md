## Verbatim quotes

### Misconceptions first (source 1)
> [03:47] "in science students don't know nothing about what we trying to teach them they actually know lots of things through their interaction with the world it just turns out that these things are wrong scientifically speaking so when you present something the student thinks they already know it and they don't really pay utmost attention they don't realize that what's being presented differs from their prior knowledge and they just get more confident in those things that they were thinking beforehand so actually a clear expository summary is worse than no instruction at all"

> [02:48] (on the clear explainer video) "they said it was clear it was concise it was easy to understand [...] on the preest they scored six out of 26 [...] The average on the post test was 6.3"

> [04:15] "I put two people in a video and I got one to voice the common misconceptions and then the the tutor would kind of indicate okay that's not quite right and they would work it out [...] no one said it was easy to understand in fact most of the time they told me it was confusing [...] their scores nearly doubled to 11 out of 26"

> [05:11] "the students who watched the dialogue invested significantly more mental effort in engaging with that presentation than students who saw only an expository summary"

> [05:39] "you always have to start with the misconceptions"

### Packaging: accurate curiosity gap, not deception (source 3)
> [05:17] "Instead of type I clickbait, my friend and YouTuber, Brady Haran suggested legitbait. [...] Instead of type II clickbait, we could use clicktrap"
> [07:09] "To be successful as a YouTuber, you need to optimize two things, watch time and the click-through rate of your videos."
> [07:34] "making the title and thumbnail is at least half the job."
> [09:49] "the title and thumbnail accurately describe what the video is about. I mean, sure, there's a curiosity gap, but you couldn't explain the whole concept in the length of the title."
> [12:02] "if I publish a video called the Collatz conjecture, the most likely people to click on it are those who already know what the Collatz conjecture is. [...] calling it "The Simplest Math Problem No One Can Solve," conveys more information about the video to everyone."
> [15:37] "What's interesting about this research is that the more clickable titles and thumbnails often better represent the content of the video."

## Pipeline takeaway
For explainers, the script should state the viewer's likely wrong belief (the "intuitive answer") before correcting it. This is testable: the grader can check that each major section names a misconception or wrong intuition before resolving it. Kallaway's "common belief → contrarian take" intro step is the same idea at hook scale.
