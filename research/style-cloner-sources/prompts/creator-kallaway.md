## Verbatim quotes

### Core principle: expectation vs reality (source 1)
> [00:56] "content psychology really boils down to one single principle expectations vers reality [...] when reality beats expectations and things go better than expected people are happy they keep watching and you win but when expectations beat reality [...] they stop watching and you lose"

### 5-step order (source 1)
> [01:51] "number one is packaging number two is outline number three is intro number four is body and number five is outro I write every single script in this exact same order"

> [01:51] "your job when you're writing the script is to make sure the first few lines of the intro confirm and ideally beat their expectations set by the title this is called click confirmation"

### Outline: novelty check plus what/why/how (source 1)
> [05:32] "uniqueness in these points can come from the actual tip itself the process of distillation how you actually say the tip or the example that you use [...] a quick outline bulleted first is a great way to gut check if you actually have something worth saying or you're just summarizing and regurgitating common things"

> [06:25] "for each point in the list I layer on the what the why and the how the what is what it is the why is why it matters and the how is how it fits in the overall puzzle of the story and again if I don't have unique points in the outline I'm not moving forward"

### Intro framework (source 1)
> [07:23] "part one is to give immediate context on what the video is about this is so the viewer can opt in and of course confirm the click"
> [07:51] "part two is to establish the common take or belief"
> [08:44] "part three as I said is by creating contrast by stating a contrarian approach"
> [09:38] "the last two steps can be done in either order but you have to do two things one establish proof for why they should trust your new approach and two give them a plan an ordered list or steps for how you're going to do it"

### Body ordering: second-best point first (source 1)
> [12:25] "the first body point of your video has to be something new and different and my cheat code for this which is not intuitive is to put your second best body point in that first slot"
> [14:41] "you want to organize your points in the body based on shock value novelty and uniqueness you want your second best point to go first your best point second your third best third and so on"

### Value loop for each body point (source 1)
> [14:13] "for each point in the body you want to include these three things I call it the value Loop one is context say what it is and explain it as simple as possible two is application say how to do it and use as many relevant examples [...] and three is framing say why this point matters and how it fits into the overall puzzle of the full story"

### Re-hooking between points (source 1)
> [15:36] "the best way to do this while maintaining Max retention is called rehooking [...] you basically want to set up these mini hooks between the end of one point and the beginning of the next one so for example you might say something like that point was super important but if you don't also couple it with this one then the magic is lost"

### Outro (source 1)
> [16:31] "leave them with a high note you want to wrap up the video in a clean way you want to summarize those points and remind them of the pain point solve"

### Three-step hook formula (source 2)
> [01:21] "the first step is what I call the context lean in the first line or two I need to do two things one I need to be super clear about what the video is actually about topic Clarity [...] the second thing I need to do is get you leaning in"
> [01:47] "establish Common Ground reference a benefit or pain point they may have use a metaphor to simplify a complex idea or tell them something so interesting it blows their mind"
> [02:40] "step two is what I call the scroll stop interjection this is a single line meant to act like a stun gun [...] by using a contrasting word I like to use the word but but you could use things like however yet although"
> [03:35] "step three this is the haymaker I call it the contrarian Snapback now the contrarian Snapback is a sentence that goes in the opposite direction of the initial lean it's still on topic but it snaps the viewer back on a different path"
> Worked example [04:03]: "the tech in the Vegas sphere is insane biggest screen ever built 20 times bigger than an IMAX but get this the screen is actually the least impressive part of the whole thing because the most impressive part is the audio"

### Other hook rules (source 2)
> [13:08] "cult hopping this is taking a known brand or a celebrity or a movement or some cultural reference and using it as a metaphor or a comparison around your point"
> [14:02] "for short form that timer is about 4 seconds for YouTube videos that timer is maybe 1 to 2 minutes so you need to find a way to compress the time to demonstrate initial value"
> [15:23] "for the hook up front you want to compress short sentences as much as you can [...] go shorter initially and then expand to medium and longer as the video extends"

### Storytelling (source 3)
> [00:28] "all great stories are like a dance between context and conflict [...] conflicts create open Loops in the brain and then context helps close those Loops" (he then cites Parker/Stone "but/therefore", see creator-south-park-but-therefore.md)
> [02:18] "tactically it's as simple as using the words but and therefore consistently throughout your script [...] if you're using the words and then it'll feel like you're piling on detail after detail"
> [04:35] (after reading Gary Provost's "This sentence has five words" passage) "write with a combination of short medium and long sentences [...] if you look at my script document I write every sentence on a separate line if you look straight down it'll look like a jagged edge and that confirms that I'm using different length sentences"
> [06:29] "write and film your videos as if you're talking to one close friend"
> [06:58] "the best place to start when you're writing a story is the end figure out what the end is going to be and then work backwards"
> [08:18] "a story lens is your unique angle or spin on a particular story"
> [09:42] "you shouldn't start your hook with some open opaque line like wait till you see this or you're never going to believe this"
