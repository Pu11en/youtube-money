`````markdown
description: Use when writing, outlining, or structuring a long-form YouTube video script (roughly 6-25 minutes) for any niche or business, or when diagnosing a video that loses viewers partway through.

# Long-Form YouTube Scripts

- Short-form wins the first 2 seconds; long-form must win the next 20 minutes.
- Retention is a chain of open loops: every section opens the next before it closes itself.
- Built on the six levers — see **storytelling-hooks** for the full psychology. One-line recap:
  1. Curiosity gap · 2. Emotional mirror · 3. Conflict engine · 4. Relatability · 5. Pattern + surprise · 6. Three-act structure.

## When to Use

- Writing a YouTube video script, tutorial, essay, story, review, or explainer.
- Planning structure/outline before scripting, or diagnosing a video that "loses people in the middle".
- Any niche: education, tech, finance, vlog, documentary, business, faith.

## Packaging First (Before Any Scripting)

- **Lock the title + thumbnail pair before outlining.** The video is written to deliver on the pack, not the other way around — a great script under a weak pack never gets watched. Use **titles-and-thumbnails** for the pair.
- **Define the target viewer** in one sentence: who exactly should click and stay (stage, situation, private worry) — not "everyone interested in X". Every relatability and stakes choice flows from this. The right viewer retained beats the most viewers clicked.
- **Intake before outlining:** confirm topic + the user's points, target runtime, audience avatar, goal (subs / sales / session time), and the next video to tease. **Always ask for the user's own points if they say they have them** — never substitute assumed points for real ones. Everything else may be assumed explicitly if the user can't respond.

## Video Skeleton (Quick Reference)

| Section | Time | Job | Lever focus |
|---------|------|-----|-------------|
| Hook | 0:00–0:30 | Stop the click-away, make a promise | 1, 4, 2 |
| Setup | 0:30–1:30 | Frame the stakes, preview the payoff | 3, 6 |
| Body (chapters) | bulk | Deliver value as a loop chain | all |
| Sag point | ~40–60% | Re-hook before attention drops | 1, 5 |
| Payoff | near end | Resolve the opening promise | 6 |
| Outro/CTA | last 15s | One next action | 1 |

- The table's timestamps assume ~10–12 min. For other lengths, scale the body proportionally but keep the hook ≤ 30s and the outro ≤ 15s regardless of runtime; the payoff can live inside the final chapter.

- When drafting hooks, re-hooks, chapter titles, or teases, read **patterns.md** (same folder) for fill-in structures — adapt, don't copy.

## The Opening (First 30 Seconds)

- **Confirm the click.** The viewer arrives holding the title/thumbnail's promise; the first line and first frame must visibly match it — restate the exact question or show the exact thing promised. A mismatch reads as bait and triggers the click-away.
- **Cold open, no throat-clearing.** Cut the "hey guys, welcome back" — start in the hook.
- **State the promise/stakes** in plain words: what the viewer will gain or avoid.
- **Open a loop you only close at the end** (the bookend).
- **Optional preview flash:** tease the most dramatic moment from later ("by the end I lost everything — here's how I got it back").
- Patterns:
  - Result-first: show the outcome, then "here's how".
  - Question gap: pose the exact question the title implied.
  - Stakes: name what's at risk if they get this wrong.

## The Re-Hook / Open-Loop Chain

- **Rule:** never close a loop without opening a new one. Retention = unbroken chain.
- Plant **forward references**: "but that's not even the strange part — I'll get to it."
- Use **micro-cliffhangers** at chapter ends.
- **Callbacks:** pay off an earlier loop mid-video to reward watching, then open another.
- Track loops explicitly while scripting — deliver an **open-loop tracker** with the outline:

  | Loop | Opens | Closes |
  |------|-------|--------|
  | "The moment nobody warns you about" (bookend) | 0:05 | payoff |
  | Chapter 1's promise | end of setup | end of ch. 1 |

## Chaptering & Pacing

- Break the video into **3–6 chapters**, each a mini three-act (setup → tension → resolution).
- One idea per chapter; name chapters as curiosity gaps, not labels ("The mistake that cost me $4k" > "Background").
- **Vary the rhythm:** alternate dense info with story/breath. Monotone pacing kills retention more than weak content.
- **Pattern interrupts** every 20–40s: B-roll, location/angle change, on-screen text, tone shift, a question to the viewer.
- Cut anything that doesn't advance a loop or pay one off.

## The Sag Point (40–60%)

- This is where most viewers drop. Treat it as a second hook.
- **Re-hook moves:** introduce the biggest twist, raise the stakes, drop a surprising fact, or restate the core promise with new urgency.
- Use **pattern + surprise** here: "Everything was working — until it wasn't."

## Payoff & Resolution

- Close the **opening loop explicitly** — viewers came for this.
- Deliver the lesson/feeling, not just facts (emotional mirror).
- **Bookend:** echo a phrase/number/image from the hook.
- Don't over-resolve — leave one resonant thought.

## Outro & CTA

- One action only (watch next / subscribe / link). Multiple CTAs = none followed.
- **Tease the next video** as a fresh curiosity gap to feed the session.
- Keep it under ~15 seconds; end-screen window.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Scripting first, packaging later | Lock title + thumbnail first; script to deliver on them |
| Opening doesn't match the title's promise | First line restates the exact promise (confirm the click) |
| Chapters as labels ("Background", "Part 2") | Name every chapter as a curiosity gap |
| Aimed at "everyone interested in X" | One-sentence target viewer; mirror *their* private experience |
| Loops tracked mentally | Written loop tracker — untracked loops get closed early or forgotten |
| Even pacing throughout | Alternate dense ↔ story/breath; plan the sag-point re-hook explicitly |

## Retention Checklist

- [ ] Title + thumbnail locked first, and the opening confirms their promise?
- [ ] Target viewer defined in one sentence?
- [ ] Cold open — no intro filler before the hook?
- [ ] Clear promise + an opening loop closed only at the end?
- [ ] Every chapter opens the next before closing itself?
- [ ] A deliberate re-hook at the 40–60% sag point?
- [ ] Pattern interrupt roughly every 20–40 seconds?
- [ ] Pacing varies (dense ↔ story/breath)?
- [ ] Payoff resolves the opening promise + bookends it?
- [ ] Single CTA + next-video tease?
- If a chapter feels flat: which lever is missing? Usually conflict or a new loop.

## Related

- **storytelling-hooks** — full six-lever psychology (read for depth).
- **titles-and-thumbnails** — the click that earns the view.
`````

`````markdown
# Long-Form Video Pattern Bank

Fill-in structures with one worked example each.

## Cold-Open Patterns (first line, confirms the click)

- **Restate + prove:** "[Title's claim, near-verbatim] — and I can prove it." — *"You're not bad at coding. You're learning it wrong. And I can prove it."*
- **Result-first:** show the outcome, then "here's how" — *"This is the exact spreadsheet that got me out of debt. By the end you'll have your own."*
- **Stakes:** "[N]% of [audience] [fail state] — and it's almost never because [expected reason]." — *"90% of people who start coding quit in a year — and it's almost never because coding is too hard."*
- **Question gap:** pose the exact question the title implied, then promise the answer with a twist — *"So why DOES your bread taste better at restaurants? It's not the oven."*

## Bookend-Loop Openers (close only at the end)

- "There's one question that predicts [outcome] better than [expected predictor]. I'll ask you it at the end."
- "By the end of this video, one number on screen will mean something completely different to you."
- "I'll tell you the exact moment it all changed — but you need the context first."

## Forward References & Micro-Cliffhangers

- "But that's not even the strange part — I'll get to it."
- "Remember this number. It comes back."
- "And this is where [thing from the hook] starts to form — one more ingredient first."
- Chapter out: "...but escaping [this problem] exposes you to something worse."

## Sag-Point Re-Hooks (40–60%)

- **The reveal-half:** pay off part of the bookend loop, immediately open the other half — *"That's the moment. Knowing when it hits is half of it — surviving it is the other half."*
- **The betrayal:** "Here's the part that should make you angry: [the system benefits from your problem]."
- **The escalation:** "Everything so far was the easy version. Here's where it gets real."
- **The callback reward:** pay off the preview flash from 0:20, then open the next loop.

## Chapter-Title Patterns (curiosity gaps, not labels)

- "The [thing] that feels like [good thing]" — *"The Trap That Feels Like Progress"*
- "Why [common solution] still isn't enough"
- "The [timeframe/number] [event]" — *"The Week-Six Collapse"*
- "What nobody does at [stage]"

## Outro / Next-Video Teases (one CTA)

- "Knowing [this video's payoff] is half of it — the other half is [next video's topic]. In this video I [specific promise] →"
- "If you're past [this video's stage], the next question is [next video's question] — I answer it here →"

`````

`````markdown
name: channel-formula
description: Use when extracting a channel's winning content formula from its own performance data — comparing top videos against flops on any YouTube/TikTok/Instagram channel in any niche (crime, tech, cricket, cozy, finance…) to codify what its audience actually comes for, or when a new video must be checked against the channel's proven pattern before scripting.

# Channel Formula

- Every channel that works has an unwritten contract with its audience: a specific promise the winners keep and the flops break. This skill makes the contract **written**.
- Generic storytelling craft is necessary but not sufficient — a technically perfect story that breaks the channel's promise still dies. **Audience expectation beats craft.**
- The output is a reusable **channel profile**: formula, title pattern, packaging rules, and retention benchmarks, extracted from the channel's own data — never from assumptions about the niche.
- Built on the six levers — see **storytelling-hooks**. Recap: 1. Curiosity gap · 2. Emotional mirror · 3. Conflict engine · 4. Relatability · 5. Pattern + surprise · 6. Three-act.

## When to Use

- A channel has some winners and some flops and nobody can articulate why.
- Before scripting for an established channel — check the premise against the formula first.
- After a flop, alongside **retention-audit** (audit finds *where* it died; this skill finds *what promise* was broken).
- Any platform, any niche: crime stories, cricket, tech reviews, cozy content, finance, faith, vlogs.

## Intake (the evidence)

- **3–5 top videos**: title, thumbnail (screenshot fine), views/velocity, and if available retention at 0:30.
- **1–3 flops** from the same format — flops are as informative as winners; the formula lives in the *difference*.
- Channel's audience in one sentence, and what viewers do with the content (background listening? active watching? saving?).
- Never derive a formula from one video or from niche stereotypes — real data only. Too few videos? Say so and mark the profile "provisional".

## Extraction Workflow

1. **Line up winners' titles side by side.** Find the shared skeleton: what kind of threat/promise/subject repeats? What resolution do they all point to? Write the pattern as a fill-in: `[recurring setup]... [recurring promise clause]`.
2. **Line up the thumbnails.** What is the focal subject in every winner? What emotion? What's never there?
3. **Name the core promise** in one sentence: what feeling does the viewer come to have? (Crime channel: "I saw the twist coming before the detective." Tech channel: "I avoided an expensive mistake." Fitness channel: "I can restart after falling off.")
4. **Autopsy the flops against it.** Which element of the promise did each flop break — premise, resolution, packaging tone, pacing? The broken element becomes a **"never" rule**.
5. **Set benchmarks from the channel's own winners** (retention at 0:30 / 2:00, typical velocity) — not from industry averages.
6. **Calibrate the levers.** Same six levers, channel-specific volume: how hot should tension run? What kind of conflict does this audience accept? How long should resolutions linger?

## Profile Output Format

Deliver (and save, if the user wants it reused) a profile containing:

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Core promise | One sentence — the feeling viewers come for |
| The formula | 2–4 numbered rules the winners share |
| Evidence table | Winners + flops with the formula kept/broken |
| Title pattern | Fill-in skeleton from the winners |
| Packaging rules | Thumbnail focal subject, emotion, never-list |
| Kill-zone rule | What the first 30s must show (confirm the click) |
| Benchmarks | Retention/velocity targets from the channel's own data |
| Lever calibration | Tension level, conflict type, resolution length |
| Never-list | Every promise-break found in a flop |

- **Where to save:** the profile is channel-private strategy — save it as a personal skill (`~/.claude/skills/<channel-name>-formula/`) or a private note, **not** in a public repo.
- The profile **overrides generic skills where they conflict** — state this in the profile so future scripting sessions respect it.

## Applying the Profile (every future video)

- Check the premise against the core promise **before** scripting — a formula-breaking premise gets reframed, not polished (a mentor-betrays-student premise on a "mentors you can trust" channel becomes "the red flags that expose a bad mentor").
- Title from the title pattern; thumbnail from the packaging rules; opening frame confirms the thumbnail.
- After publish, run **retention-audit**; feed every new winner/flop back into the profile. The formula is living — two lines of update per video.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Formula from niche stereotypes ("crime = shocking") | Extract from THIS channel's winners only |
| Only studying winners | Flops define the never-list — the formula is in the difference |
| One video treated as a pattern | 3+ winners minimum; otherwise mark provisional |
| Formula written once, never updated | Update after every publish via retention-audit |
| Channel profile committed to a public repo | Private skill/note — it's competitive strategy |
| Formula treated as a cage | It constrains *premise and packaging*, not creativity within them |

## Formula Checklist

- [ ] 3+ winners and ≥1 flop examined?
- [ ] Core promise stated in one sentence?
- [ ] Title pattern written as a fill-in skeleton?
- [ ] Every flop mapped to a broken rule (never-list)?
- [ ] Benchmarks taken from the channel's own data?
- [ ] Levers calibrated to the audience's tension tolerance?
- [ ] Profile saved privately for reuse?

## Related

- **retention-audit** — post-publish diagnosis that feeds this profile.
- **titles-and-thumbnails** / **long-form-youtube** / **storytelling-hooks** — the generic craft the profile calibrates.
`````

`````markdown
---
name: retention-audit
description: Use when diagnosing why a published video underperformed — reading a YouTube retention graph, comparing a weak video against a winner, or mapping audience drop-off points to script beats. For writing the next script, use storytelling-hooks or long-form-youtube.
---

# Retention Audit

- A retention graph is the story's autopsy: every drop is a moment the script broke a promise, every spike is a beat viewers rewound to see again.
- Audit by **zones**, not by staring at the whole line — each zone has its own failure causes and its own fix.
- Always diagnose **against a baseline**: the same channel's best comparable video (same length/format). Absolute numbers mislead; the gap between winner and loser is the finding.
- Built on the six levers — see **storytelling-hooks**. Recap: 1. Curiosity gap · 2. Emotional mirror · 3. Conflict engine · 4. Relatability · 5. Pattern + surprise · 6. Three-act.

## When to Use

- A video got clicks but died (decent CTR, low average view duration).
- YouTube stopped recommending a video after its first test batch.
- Comparing a flop against the channel's winners to find what broke.
- Any niche; needs the retention graph (screenshot is fine), the script or beat outline, and ideally one winner's graph.

## Intake (Before Auditing)

- Get: this video's retention graph + length, the script or beat list with rough timestamps, CTR and impressions if available, and a winner's graph from the same channel/format.
- Also get the **thumbnail + title + the video's first frame** — the #1 retention killer lives in that triangle, not in the script.
- Missing the winner baseline? Use the gray "typical retention" band as a weak substitute and say so.
- **No graph, no audit.** Never estimate or invent retention numbers — ask for the screenshot; offer only hypothesis-level guesses clearly labeled as such.

## The Five Zones (Quick Reference)

| Zone | Where | Healthy sign | Failure means |
|------|-------|--------------|---------------|
| Cliff | 0:00–0:30 | ≥65–70% still watching | packaging↔opening mismatch (confirm-the-click failure) |
| Intro | 0:30–2:00 | slope flattening | throat-clearing, promise not restated, stakes missing |
| Body | 2:00–sag | tracks/above typical band; small spikes | a loop closed without opening the next; pacing monotone |
| Sag | ~40–60% of runtime | a visible re-hook bump or held line | no planned re-hook; mid-video drift |
| End | last 10% | gentle taper | fine unless a cliff — outro started too early |

- Timestamp boundaries assume ~10–12 min videos; scale the intro/body boundaries proportionally for other lengths — the 0:00–0:30 cliff stays fixed regardless of runtime.
- **Spikes = rewatch beats.** Mark them: those are the moments that worked — reuse their structure in future scripts.
- **The most common pattern:** steep cliff + healthy body = the *video* was fine, the *first minute* betrayed the click. Fix packaging/opening, not the story.

## Audit Workflow

1. **Overlay the comparison.** Note the winner-vs-loser gap at 0:30, 1:00, 2:00. If the gap is born in the first minute, the diagnosis is almost never "bad content."
2. **Check the click triangle.** Thumbnail promise vs title promise vs actual first frame/line. Any mismatch = the cliff's cause. (Winner channels show the *same world* in thumbnail and frame one.)
3. **Map drops to beats.** Put the script's beats on the timeline; for each visible dip, name the beat that was playing. Ask: did this beat close a loop without opening one? Sag without a re-hook? Tension the audience didn't come for?
4. **Check the channel formula.** Compare the flop's premise against the winners' shared pattern (subject type, resolution type, emotional promise) — use **channel-formula** to extract it if it isn't written down yet. A technically good story that breaks the channel's core promise still dies — audience expectation beats craft.
5. **Mark the spikes.** List rewatched moments and what structural move each used.
6. **Deliver findings** — for each: timestamp zone, what the graph shows, the script beat responsible, the cause, the fix. End with the single highest-leverage change for the next video, and feed the result back into the channel's profile (**channel-formula**) if one exists.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Judging by end-retention or AVD alone | Zone-by-zone against a winner — the cliff decides distribution |
| Blaming the script for a 0:30 cliff | Check the click triangle first; the body may be healthy |
| Ignoring spikes | Spikes are free R&D — codify what those beats did |
| Auditing without a baseline | Same-channel winner first; typical band only as fallback |
| One flop = change everything | Change the diagnosed zone only; keep what the graph says worked |
| Findings without next-video actions | Every finding ends in a concrete instruction for the next script |

## Audit Checklist

- [ ] Winner baseline (or typical band) overlaid?
- [ ] 0:30 / 1:00 / 2:00 gaps measured?
- [ ] Click triangle (thumbnail–title–first frame) checked for mismatch?
- [ ] Every visible dip mapped to a named script beat?
- [ ] Premise checked against the channel's winning formula?
- [ ] Rewatch spikes catalogued?
- [ ] One highest-leverage fix named for the next video?

## Related
`````
