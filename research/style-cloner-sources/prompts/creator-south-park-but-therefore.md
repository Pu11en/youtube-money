# Trey Parker & Matt Stone (South Park) — "But / Therefore" rule

- Source: MTVU "Stand In" guest lecture at NYU (2011). Re-upload used for the transcript: "Writing Advice from Matt Stone & Trey Parker @ NYU | MTVU's "Stand In"", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGUNqq3jVLg (1.32M views; yt-dlp transcript 2026-09-18)
- Proof of results: South Park creators, with multiple Emmy wins (the lecture intro says "Emmy award-winning"). YouTube proof: Kallaway quotes this clip as his top storytelling rule and says his Stanley Cup video uses "four but/then conflict loops in the first 30 seconds" (see creator-kallaway.md, source 3, [00:56]–[02:46]).
- Ready prompt published? No. The rule is easy to turn into a check.

## Verbatim quotes
> [00:56] "we can take these beats which are basically the beats of your outline. And if the words and then belong between those beats, you're basically you got you got something pretty boring. What should happen between every beat that you've written down is either the word therefore or but"

> [00:56] "you come up with an idea and it's like okay this happens, right? And then this happens. No, no, no. should be this happens and therefore this happens but this happens therefore this happens"

> [01:53] "it's those those two but because therefore that gives you the causation between each beat and that makes that that's a story."

> [00:29] "Each individual scene has to work as a kind of funny sketch. You don't want one scene that's just like, well, what what was the point of that scene?"

## Pipeline takeaway
This is the most machine-checkable structural rule in the set. For each pair of adjacent beats in the outline, classify the link as BUT (complication), THEREFORE (consequence), or AND-THEN (sequence only). Score = share of links that are BUT/THEREFORE. Also store the competitor's BUT:THEREFORE ratio and spacing as a style-DNA field.
